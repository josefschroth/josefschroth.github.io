Josef Schroth

Hello and welcome! I work as an economist at Bank of Canada.

My research interests are in macro-finance and corporate finance.

Specific topics are bank regulation, capital structure and disclosure choice.

Research

Publications

Macroprudential regulation and banks’ supply of liquidity services

2026 · Journal of Financial Economics

Paper Presentation

When banks retain earnings to guard against potential losses from high-risk activities, the additional balance sheet space results in mispricing of low-risk activities. This distorts banks’ franchise values down and lowers their ability to retain earnings, leading to lower financial stability.

Capital Flows and Growth across Developing Countries

2023 · Journal of International Money and Finance

Paper Short presentation

Foreign direct investment inflows and overall capital outflows are positively related to growth across developing countries when consumer borrowing limits are tighter in developing countries than in developed countries.

Macroprudential Policy with Capital Buffers

2021 · Journal of Monetary Economics

Paper Presentation Non-technical presentation Blog post

Optimal capital buffers should be large eventually but small in the aftermath of financial crises such that their anticipation does not adversely affect market participants’ expectations about bank moral hazard during crises.

On the Distributional Effects of Bank Bailouts

2021 · Review of Economic Dynamics

Paper Presentation Non-technical presentation Blog post

An important class of financial crisis policy responses provides temporary support to solvent institutions, following Bagehot’s principle. The paper analyzes general equilibrium effects from such policy responses and finds that they redistribute from poor to wealthy households.

Managerial Compensation and Stock Price Manipulation

2018 · Journal of Accounting Research

Paper

Firm owners pay managers to run firms, but not all pay depends on performance. Some of it depends on how managers communicate with market participants.

Optimal Intermediary Rents

2016 · American Economic Journal: Macroeconomics

Paper

Regulators should raise bank profitability following a financial crisis to alleviate concerns about bank moral hazard during the crisis. Crises are then less deep but recoveries also take longer.

Paper Short presentation

Macroprudential and resolution policies in banking unions

2023

Paper

To avoid moral hazard of national macroprudential regulators, the usage of resolution funding should be positively correlated across member countries of a banking union. Optimal policies imply time-varying financial stability and economic output that is on average higher net of resolution costs.

Policy contributions

Unintended consequences of liquidity regulation

2025 · Bank of Canada

Read note

When a bank holds a lot of safe assets, it is well situated to deal with funding stress. But when all banks hold a lot of safe assets, a pecuniary externality implies that their (wholesale) funding costs increase. This reduces banks’ ability to hold capital buffers and thus, paradoxically, increases the frequency of funding stress.

Anticipating changes in bank capital buffer requirements

2025 · Bank of Canada

Read note

Time-varying capital buffer requirements are a powerful tool that allow bank regulators to avoid severe financial stress without the cost of imposing very high levels of capital. However, this tool is only effective if banks understand how it is used. I present a model that banks and financial market participants can use to anticipate how time-varying capital buffer requirements change over time.
